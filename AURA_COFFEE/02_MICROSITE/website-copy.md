# 03 — THE HERD

---

## Our best farmers have four legs.

Fifty-two Malnad Gidda — small, hardy, indigenous to these hills, and grazing them for centuries before anyone thought to write it down.

They eat the same one hundred and fifty acres the coffee grows in. What they take from a block, they give back to it.

Almost everything this soil is fed begins in their bodies.

---

They live a real life on this ground.

**How it is actually made**

At first light the dung is gathered fresh, weighed, and split three ways — some to the compost pits, some to the microbial brews, some packed into horns. The urine, most concentrated at dawn, is measured into the same system.

Fourteen numbered pits, turned by hand about forty-five minutes a day, matured ninety days into something dark, crumbly and earth-smelling. Rolled into balls and dug in at the root of a named plant. `[PROOF]`

One hundred and fifty-four thousand litres of brew a year — each barrel stirred by hand, one direction then the other, a vortex built and broken so the good organisms outcompete the rest. `[PROOF]`

Aura does not buy fertility. It makes it.

> `[PROOF]` — Cow Pat Pit
> ~2,420 kg a year across 14 numbered pits. 90-day cycle. Dung, crushed eggshell for calcium, ground basalt for minerals, and the BD 502–507 compost preparations.
> Each ball is logged to the individual plant it was dug in at.
> Status: Estate record. `[NEEDS PROOF]` — confirm actual vs planned volume, and plant-ID coverage.

> `[PROOF]` — Jeevamrit

> Volume planned backwards from a biodynamic calendar for 150 acres; stock registers track every batch against it.

# 05 — THE LIGHT

---

## Measure first. Prune later. Validate afterwards.

Shade has been managed here by eye for three centuries. A contractor reads the canopy, judges what to take, directs the cut.

Done by feel, it opens the canopy unevenly and distorts a tree that will stand another fifty years. Some coffee scorched, some starved, inside the same block.

---

*Second beat:*

In the 2026 pre-monsoon season, two forestry interns — Nayana and Jagadeshwari — walked the blocks with lux meters before a branch came down. `[PROOF]`

Five clusters an acre. Ten readings each. Fifty observations, resolved into three zones: dense, medium, open.

The cut answers the zone, not the block.

Then the wood stays where it fell, and every major cut is dressed with cow dung preparation and turmeric.

**And one rule above all others**

No untested material touches the soil.

Every batch is read in the estate's own lab before and after — pH, conductivity, microbial colony counts, *Trichoderma*, *Pseudomonas*. `[PROOF]`

A batch that comes back wrong is corrected or held. Never sprayed.

Ninety days later, the block it went onto is read again.

> `[PROOF]` — Batch testing
> Pit numbers for the dung, batch numbers for the brews. 60–90 day cycles by preparation. Tested before and after, alongside the sensory reads the team trusts — smell, colour, surface activity.
> When a reading drifts, Dr Arun sets the correction: hold it longer, add this much water, feed in this much brew.

# 07 — WHAT WE CAN'T PROVE

*The movement no competitor will copy. Same type size, weight and colour as everything around it. The moment it looks like fine print, it becomes fine print.*

---

## And here is what we don't know.

The Solera method borrows from sherry, where a living culture is genuinely kept alive across casks for decades. We carry a fraction of a fermenting batch forward into fresh cherry, hoping to seed the same continuity.

Can a coffee ferment actually hold a house culture from one season into the next? We do not know. Coffee ferments are short, and nobody has shown a strain that survives the gap.

We are testing it, year by year. We would rather tell you that than sell you the metaphor.

# 08 — THE SIX

*Where the argument becomes appetite.*

---

## The same cherry, picked the same morning, becomes six different coffees.

Only fully ripe cherries are plucked — minimum ninety-five per cent. Every float is removed.

Then the decisions begin. Sealed or open. Twenty-four hours or forty-eight. Banana leaves or none. Mucilage on, or washed away.

Terroir alone does not explain the spread between them. Terroir expressed through technique does.

---

**Six lots. One harvest.**

**001 · Anaerobic Natural**
Forty-eight hours sealed. The cherry's own microbes do the work — the simplest method there is.

**002 · Dry Osmosis**
Dried to forty-five per cent moisture first, then fermented. Our cupping table calls it blueberry, fig, raisin.

**003 · Red Honey — "Liquid Gold"**
Pulped with the mucilage left on. Five days thick drying, then twenty-five thin. Honey, caramel, nut. It was named at its first public tasting, and the name stuck.

**004 · Banana Wash**
Twenty-four hours anaerobic, then layered under banana leaves for forty-eight more. The leaves bring their own wild yeasts. The most distinctly Indian lot on the estate.

**005 · Solera Maceration** · *flagship*
Half of an actively fermenting batch carried forward into freshly picked cherry, the way a sherry cellar carries its culture. Alternating ferment and rest.

**006 · Solera Wash** · *rarest*
Twenty-four hours, a three-day soak, hand-washed. The highest sugar of any lot at twenty-three per cent Brix — and then everything non-essential stripped away.

> pH read every 15 minutes through the ferment. Temperature three times daily. Ferment stopped at pH 4.2 — by the number, not by nose.
> Status: Estate record. `[NEEDS PROOF]` — harvest year to be confirmed.

**The honest footnote** *(set smaller, in the founder's hand)*

We will be honest about our own romance. A sealed ferment or a chosen yeast moves a cup by a point or three — real, measurable, worth chasing. But altitude and variety set the ceiling. The ferment only decides how much of that ceiling the cup reaches.

Anyone who tells you the process alone makes the coffee is skipping the harder half of the story.
