# Sound Direction
## Aura Regenerative Coffee · 2:30

**The principle:** the estate scores itself. Almost every sound in this film was recorded on the ground it is about. There is one musical element, it enters once, and it lasts nine seconds.

A film this quiet lives or dies on its sound. Budget accordingly — the sound mix matters more here than the grade.

---

## THE FOUR LAYERS

### 1. Room tone — the spine
Every scene carries its own room. The interiors (cup, wet mill, shed) are close and dry. The exteriors are wide and alive. The cuts between them should be felt as much as seen — the audience should know they have moved indoors before they have worked out what they are looking at.

The film opens on a room with a building in it, faintly. It closes on the same room. That symmetry is the audio version of the circle.

### 2. Estate ambience — the world
Recorded, not libraried. The Western Ghats at 3,600 feet has a specific and busy soundscape: cicadas, hornbills, bulbuls, wind moving through four separate canopy layers at different speeds, and water somewhere most of the time.

**The arrival at 0:14 is the film's biggest audio moment.** The first fourteen seconds are almost silent — a cup, a room. Then the match cut to the drying bed and the entire estate comes in at once, loud, and settles. It should be startling. Mix it hot and pull it down over three seconds.

### 3. Physical detail — the argument
This is where the film is actually made. Every one of these is a close-recorded, foreground sound:

| Time | Sound |
|---|---|
| 0:06 | Ceramic on wood. A chair. Someone breathing out. |
| 0:14 | Beans moving under a wooden rake — dry, granular, rhythmic |
| 0:32 | The ferment: gas escaping, a wet slick, a single drip |
| 0:44 | Glass, liquid, one small electronic tone from the meter |
| 0:52 | Cloth, leaves, the dull knock of cherry into a basket |
| 1:14 | Soil, leaf litter, one breath |
| 1:30 | **The stir** — water turning, wood against steel, effort in the breathing |
| 2:04 | An animal breathing. A bell, once, far off |
| 2:12 | Ceramic. Breath. The room from 0:06, returned |

**The stir is the film's centrepiece in sound as much as in picture.** Twenty-two seconds of one man moving water with a pole. Record it with a shotgun on the pole and a contact mic on the barrel, and mix it so the vortex has low end. It should feel like weather.

### 4. Human voice — sparse and untranslated
Kannada, distant, under the picking sequence at 0:52. **Not subtitled.** The audience is not meant to understand it; they are meant to understand that people are working.

The voiceover sits above all four layers, close-mic'd and dry, with almost no reverb. It should sound like it is in the room with the viewer rather than in the film.

## MUSIC

**There are nine seconds of music in this film.**

One low sustained note — a bowed double bass, a harmonium drone, or a struck singing bowl left to decay. It enters under the word *"Yes"* at 2:24 and decays through the end card.

That is all.

**No score under the body of the film.** No ambient pad, no rising strings, no piano, no percussion bed, no "emotional" cue at the cow. The temptation to score the stir will be enormous and must be refused — the stir already has rhythm, and adding music to it converts a real thing into a commercial.

**Why the single note works:** the audience has been listening to two and a half minutes of unscored reality. The first musical sound in the film lands like an answer. It is doing the same job the endline does, and it arrives at the same moment.

**If the note is wrong, use nothing.** A silent end card is better than a scored one.

---

## THE THREE SILENCES

Silence here means *no ambience, no music, no voice* — only room tone at a very low floor. All three are load-bearing.

| Time | Length | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1:14 — 1:22** | 8 sec | After *"made by an animal."* The longest hold in the film. The audience is doing the arithmetic, and the sound has to get out of the way while they do it. |
| **1:22 — 1:30** | 8 sec | The fungi / worm / microbe sequence. The audio floor drops so far the audience physically leans in. |
| **2:20 — 2:24** | 4 sec | Cut to black, cut the sound with it. Total. Then *"Is that a thing?"* out of nothing. |

Approximately 48 seconds of the 150 have no voice on them at all.

---

## THE MIX

**Dynamic range is the whole point.** Do not compress this film to broadcast loudness. The gap between the quietest moment (1:22) and the loudest (0:14, the estate arriving) should be genuinely large, and the viewer should have to lean in at least twice.

**Deliverables**
- 5.1 for cinema and festival
- Stereo for web and social
- **A mobile-optimised mix.** Most people will watch this on a phone in a room with other noise, and the quiet mix will simply vanish. The mobile version needs the ambience 3–4 dB up and the silences shortened by about a second each — a different mix, not a limiter on the same one.
- **Subtitles on every version**, and open captions on the vertical social cuts.

**Loudness targets:** −24 LUFS for the master, −16 LUFS for the mobile and social mixes.

---

## RECORDING NOTES FOR THE SHOOT

Record clean audio on **every** setup, including the shots that will run muted in the final cut. Half the sounds in this list will come from takes nobody planned to use.

**Kit:** a shotgun, two lavs (for the shed), a contact mic (for the barrel), and a stereo pair for ambience. Record thirty minutes of clean estate ambience at three times of day — dawn, mid-morning, and dusk — with the camera off and nobody moving. That library is worth more than any additional camera setup.

**The one thing not to forget:** wild-track the stir for a full five minutes, separately from the picture take. The edit will want more of it than the shot provides.
