## THE SCRIPT, WITH DIRECTION

**0:22 — over the drying bed, top-down**
> *This is the last thing that happened.*

Flat. Almost thrown away. This is the line that tells the audience the film runs backwards, and it must not sound like it knows that. **Do not land it.**

---

**0:38 — over the ferment, foam lifting**
> *Before it, a week in the dark.*

Quieter than the first line. A slight smile is allowed here and nowhere else in the first half.

---

**0:52 — over hands picking, cherry by cherry**
> *Before that, a morning*
> *when somebody decided this cherry was ready*
> *and that one wasn't.*

The only three-clause line in the film. Let the middle clause hang half a beat before the last one. It should sound like someone remembering, not reciting.

---

**1:06 — over light moving through the canopy**
> *Before that, light.*
> *We measure it before we cut.*

Two sentences, two different registers — the first almost reverent, the second entirely practical. That gear change is the whole personality of the brand in three seconds. **Do not smooth it out.**

---

**1:12 — the turn. Over the ball of compost going in at the root**
> *Everything you just watched*
> *was made by an animal.*

The most important line in the film. Deliver it as a fact, not a reveal. No emphasis on *animal*. The picture does the work; if the voice pushes, the moment dies.

Then **eight seconds of nothing** — the longest silence in the film.

---

**1:34 — over the cow, held**
> *She eats the same ground the coffee grows in.*
> *What she takes from it, she gives back.*

Warmer. The only line in the film that is about a relationship rather than a process. *She*, not *they* — this is one animal, on screen, being looked at.

---

**1:52 — over the loop closing: grass, dung, barrel, soil, root, cherry**
> *Nothing comes in.*
> *Nothing leaves.*

Two hard stops. The edit cuts on each full stop.

---

**2:04 — over the vortex in the barrel, the last time we see it**
> *We don't buy fertility.*
> *We make it.*

The proudest line in the film, and it must be said like the least proud. Plain, slightly tired, the voice of someone who did it this morning.

---

**2:12 — over the cup, returning**
> *Coffee that leaves the land*
> *better than it found it.*

The definition, arriving after the proof. First time in the film anything is defined.

---

**2:18 — black**
> *Is that a thing?*

A genuine question. Amused. As if asked by someone who has been asked it a hundred times and still finds it funny.

---

**2:24 — black**
> *Yes. It is.*

Two words, a full stop between them. Then the title.

**This is the last thing anybody says.** No sign-off, no web address read aloud, no "visit us". The card carries it.

## CASTING THE VOICE

**First choice — Arvind Singh.** The founder, unperformed. The lines are already written in his register: *"I came to 150 acres knowing nothing about farming. That turned out to be the advantage."* Someone who talks like that should read this.

**Second choice — a worker on the estate**, in English or in Kannada with subtitles. If Kannada, the film gets substantially better and substantially harder to place internationally. Worth testing.

**Do not cast a voice artist.** The moment this sounds like a commercial, the honesty in line 5 and line 8 stops being credible, and those two lines are the entire film.

**Direction for the session:** record it sitting down, in a room, close mic, no booth. Ask for four passes: one as written, one faster, one as if explaining to a friend who is slightly sceptical, and one at half volume. The fourth pass is usually the one.

---

## WHAT THE VOICEOVER NEVER DOES

- Never describes what is on screen.
- Never says *sustainable*, *journey*, *passion*, *commitment*, *ecosystem*, *biodiversity*, or *regenerative* until 2:12.
- Never names the estate, the breed, the preparations, the cultivars or a single number.
- Never asks the viewer to do anything.

Every one of those things is available in the microsite and the deck. The film's job is to make somebody want to look them up.
