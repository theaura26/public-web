# Interview Questions
## For the people who actually do this

**The purpose of these interviews is not quotes for a website.** It is to find the three or four concrete, physical, slightly odd details that no strategist could invent — the leopard, the forty-five minutes of stirring, the cherry that was ready and the one that wasn't. Those details are what make the difference between a brand that describes regenerative farming and a brand that is obviously doing it.

**Method.** Record audio, always. Ask the question, then stop talking. The answer after the pause is the one worth having. Never ask anything answerable with *yes*.

---

## ARVIND SINGH — FOUNDER

### The origin
1. You came to 150 acres knowing nothing about farming, and you've said that turned out to be the advantage. What did you do in the first month that someone who knew farming would never have done?
2. What was the first thing the land told you that you didn't want to hear?
3. What did you get wrong in year one that you'd now defend to anyone?

### The belief
4. When did you stop thinking in quarters and start thinking in generations — was there a specific day?
5. You've written that the lunar charts and the talk of the earth breathing are poetry, and that poetry has never survived a trial. That's an unusual thing for someone doing biodynamics to publish. Why say it?
6. What would have to be true for you to stop doing the biodynamic preparations altogether?

### The coffee
7. Describe the first cup from this estate that surprised you. Where were you, and what did it taste like?
8. Which of the six lots do you actually reach for?
9. If a roaster told you the process alone makes the coffee, what would you say back?
10. What is the estate not good at yet?

### The Experience — `[BLOCKS PUBLICATION]`
11. Walk me through what someone actually does here in September. Then December. Then January.
12. Who are those three months for, and who are they not for?
13. What do you want someone to be thinking about on the drive back down the hill?

### The uncomfortable ones
14. You're not certified organic or biodynamic. Does that cost you anything?
15. What's the strongest argument against what you're doing?
16. If the Solera carry-forward turns out not to work across seasons, what changes?

## DR ARUN — ESTATE SCIENTIST

*The most valuable interview in this list. He is the person who can turn assertions into evidence.*

1. Take me through a batch that failed. What did the reading say, what did you tell them to do, and how did it come back?
2. What do you test that most estates don't?
3. What can you see in the soil now that you couldn't see two years ago?
4. When the hand and the lab disagree, who wins?
5. What's the hardest thing to measure here?
6. Ninety days after an application, what does a good result actually look like on the page?
7. What would you need to prove the Solera culture carries across seasons? What would that experiment be?
8. Is there anything the estate believes that you're not yet convinced by?
9. What's the number you watch that nobody asks about?

**Also ask for, in writing:** one dated lab report, one soil before/after pair, and the current status of the fourteen ecological monitoring programmes. See [content-gaps.md](content-gaps.md) items 6, 7 and 10.

## THE PERSON WHO STIRS THE BARREL

*Whoever does this daily. This is the film's centrepiece; the interview should be conducted at the barrel, in Kannada if that's easier, and subtitled.*

1. How long have you been doing this?
2. Show me — what are you actually doing with the pole? Why one direction and then the other?
3. How do you know when it's ready? What are you looking at?
4. What does a good batch smell like? What does a bad one smell like?
5. How many barrels this week?
6. What happens if you skip a day?
7. Does the work change in the monsoon?

## THE CARETAKER WHO KNOWS THE HERD

1. How many of them do you know by name?
2. Which one is the difficult one?
3. Tell me about the one that got attacked by the leopard.
4. What do you notice in the morning that tells you an animal isn't right?
5. What's different about a Malnad Gidda compared to other cattle you've worked with?
6. What happens on a day when everything goes well?

## NAYANA AND JAGADESHWARI — THE LUX SURVEY

1. What were you actually asked to do, and had anyone done it before here?
2. Fifty readings an acre — walk me through one acre.
3. What surprised you in the numbers?
4. Was there a block where the reading disagreed with what your eyes said?
5. What happened after you handed the data over — did the cutting actually follow it?

## A PICKER, DURING HARVEST

1. How do you decide a cherry is ready?
2. How many do you leave behind on a branch?
3. What does a good picking morning feel like?
4. How is this estate different from others you've picked on?

## A ROASTER WHO HAS BOUGHT THE COFFEE — `[IF ONE EXISTS]`

1. What did you expect, and what did you get?
2. How do the six lots behave differently in the roaster?
3. What did you tell your customers about it?
4. What would you want to know that the estate hasn't told you?
5. Did you look at the traceability record? Did anyone ask you to?

**If no such roaster exists yet, that is itself a finding** — and it changes how the trade material should be written.

## WHAT TO LISTEN FOR

Flag anything that is:

- **Physical** — a smell, a weight, a temperature, a length of time
- **Specific** — a name, a number, a date, a block
- **Slightly wrong** — an admission, a failure, a thing that didn't work
- **Funny without trying to be** — the best material in this brand is dry, not witty

Discard anything that sounds like it came from a brochure, including when it comes from the founder. Especially then.
